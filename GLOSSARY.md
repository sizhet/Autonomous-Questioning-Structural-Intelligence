# Glossary — Autonomous Questioning Structural Intelligence (AQSI)

**Release:** v1.0.0 (theory-first research framework)  
**Status:** Proposed terminology and interface vocabulary; not a claim of implemented or empirically validated capabilities.  
**Scope:** AQSI-001–010, SPEC-001–004, CASE-001–003, EXT-001–003.

## Purpose and conventions

This glossary provides consistent meanings for the principal terms used across the AQSI research repository. Terms describe **proposed research constructs**, unless otherwise indicated. An algorithmic score is an estimate, not a verified scientific result; a proposed interface is not a deployed implementation. Terms such as *must* and *should* are normative only when cited within a specification's stated conformance scope.

**Notation:** `q_i` is a Question Unit; `h_ia` is its structural factor `a`; `I(q_i → q_j)` is a directed estimated influence; `H_t` is the historical question record at time `t`; `P` is a research portfolio. The symbol `→` expresses direction, not causality by itself.

## A — D

### Action Authorization
An explicit permission to carry out a particular exploration or external action, subject to identity, scope, budget, and safety constraints. **Selection of a question does not confer authorization.** See *Exploration Contract*, *Operational Control Plane*.

### AQSI (Autonomous Questioning Structural Intelligence)
A proposed research architecture for discovering, representing, evaluating, selecting, exploring, and relaying research questions, including the possibility of improving those processes through independently validated changes. AQSI is a framework hypothesis, not an assertion of autonomous scientific discovery or ASI.

### Association-Driven Question Discovery
Candidate-question generation from relationships among concepts, attributes, methods, evidence, or domains. Associations may be weak, directional, uncertain, or cross-granularity; they require validation before being treated as useful transfers.

### Biomimetic Question Discovery
Engineering inspiration drawn from functional patterns of human questioning, such as noticing anomalies, gaps, contradictions, analogies, and unresolved consequences. It does not claim to reproduce human cognition or neural mechanisms.

### Breakthrough Readiness
A provisional estimate that a research direction may be close to resolving an important gap, based on structural dependencies, available methods, evidence, and historical progress. It is uncertain, context-dependent, and must not be presented as a reliable breakthrough forecast without validation.

### Candidate Question
A proposed research question not yet accepted for exploration. A candidate may be generated by a language model, structural graph operation, historical analogy, human input, or relay.

### Canonical Question Unit (QU)
A persistable, versioned research object containing a question's identity, typed structure, provenance, evidence references, assessment references, lifecycle state, and governance metadata. A QU is distinct from its textual prompt and from any individual exploration run. See `SPEC-001-Canonical-Question-Unit-Contract.md`.

### Collective Structural Learning
Improvement in a group or ecosystem's reusable research structures through traceable exchange and evaluation of questions, methods, evidence, counter-evidence, and outcomes. Multi-agent conversation alone is not sufficient evidence of collective learning.

### Concept Association Graph
A graph of concept and other structural-factor relationships used to discover candidate links and questions. AQSI does **not** restrict this graph to concept-to-concept or first-order edges.

### Conformance
Demonstrated satisfaction of an explicit interface or governance contract by a particular artifact, process, or implementation. Conformance is not equivalent to scientific correctness or research impact.

### Control Plane
A layer that defines policy, priorities, resource allocation, coordination, or operational constraints for question selection and exploration. AQSI distinguishes strategic, portfolio, tactical, and operational layers.

### Counter-Evidence
Evidence that challenges a hypothesis, relation, proposed transfer, scoring estimate, or claimed improvement. Counter-evidence is retained with provenance rather than silently discarded.

### Cross-Domain Question Transfer
The creation of a target-domain question using a typed, qualified mapping from source-domain structures or research methods. **Question transfer is not answer transfer** and does not establish that source-domain results hold in the target domain.

### Cross-Structural Co-Evolution (CSCE)
A research hypothesis in which changes to AQSI and related structural systems (such as UTN, scoring, or relay mechanisms) create mutually useful, independently evaluated improvements. Mere interaction between systems is not verified co-evolution.

### Delta / Structural Delta
A typed difference between related question structures, methods, contexts, or versions, expressed relative to a shared core or baseline. A delta may be beneficial, neutral, harmful, or unknown.

### Delayed Relay Value
The possible later contribution of a question through descendants, transferred methods, new evidence, or subsequent discoveries. This value is difficult to attribute and should not be conflated with immediate outcomes.

### Directional Influence
An estimated effect or enabling relationship from one question or structural factor to another. In general `I(q_i → q_j) ≠ I(q_j → q_i)`. Directional influence is not automatically a causal effect.

### Discovery–Exploration–Relay Loop
The proposed cycle in which questions are generated, represented, evaluated, selected, explored under contract, and relayed into historical records and future question discovery.

## E — H

### Evidence Record
A provenance-bearing account of an observation, source, test, result, or reason relevant to a question. Records should distinguish empirical observation from model-generated interpretation or speculation.

### Evidence Dependence
The condition in which multiple observations, reports, or scores share underlying sources or mechanisms. Dependence must be considered to avoid counting repeated evidence as independent confirmation.

### Explicit Leftover
A documented unresolved issue, uncertainty, limitation, missing dependency, or residual after an exploration. Leftovers are first-class inputs to future question discovery and relay.

### Exploration
An authorized attempt to investigate a Question Unit using specified methods, tools, and evidence criteria. Exploration may produce an answer, partial result, negative result, new question, or unresolved residual.

### Exploration Contract (EC)
A scoped specification for an exploration: question reference, objectives, permitted methods and resources, authorization, evidence requirements, stop conditions, and expected output artifacts. See `AQSI-006-Question-Units-and-Exploration-Contracts.md` and `SPEC-003-Question-Relay-and-Exploration-Lifecycle.md`.

### Exploration Lifecycle
The state progression of an individual exploration run, distinct from the lifecycle of its Question Unit. Example stages include proposed, authorized, running, evidence collected, reviewed, and closed; exact transitions are contract-defined.

### Factor / Structural Influence Factor
A typed feature or relation contributing to the estimated influence between questions. Factors can concern concepts, attributes, methods, evidence, constraints, residuals, historical outcomes, and transfer points.

### Folding / Influence Folding
An explicitly specified aggregation of factor-level evidence or scores into a question-pair, question-level, or portfolio-level assessment. The aggregation operator, weights, missing-data policy, and evidence dependencies should be recorded. See *UCPSF*.

### Gap-Driven Question Discovery
Generation of questions from missing knowledge, incomplete capabilities, contradictions, or unaddressed dependencies.

### Goal-Open Question Scoring
Scoring a collection of candidate questions without prespecifying one concrete target question. It may still employ research values, evidence standards, constraints, and human policy; **goal-open does not mean value-free or policy-free**.

### Governance Boundary
A stated limit on authority, data access, resource use, policy modification, or external actions. AQSI's autonomous question discovery does not imply autonomous strategic sovereignty.

### Historical Question Intelligence (HQI)
A reusable structure of past questions, assessments, explorations, failures, successes, descendants, and feedback used to evaluate new questions and update research methods. Historical patterns are imperfect and may encode selection bias.

### Historical Question Repository
A provenance-preserving store of Question Units, assessments, exploration outcomes, and relay relationships; it need not be implemented as a single database.

## I — P

### Influence Matrix
A directed pairwise representation `M_ij = I(q_i → q_j)` for a candidate set. Entries may include uncertainty and explicit unknown status; missing values must not silently be treated as zero.

### LLM-First Question Generation
A path in which a language model proposes candidate questions before structural typing, comparison, and evaluation. It is complementary to *Structure-First Question Generation*.

### Meta-Question
A question about the processes or structures used to discover, represent, score, select, explore, or relay questions, including questions about AQSI itself.

### Meta-Question Discovery (MQD)
The process of identifying useful meta-questions from performance gaps, failure patterns, conformance issues, or alternative research methods. Generating meta-questions does not establish self-improvement.

### Multi-Agent Relay
Exchange of structured question and research artifacts among agents or researchers with explicit roles, permissions, and provenance. Agents' agreement is not independent verification.

### Multi-Granularity Structural Representation
A representation allowing questions to refer to entities and relationships at different scales, such as domains, concepts, attributes, methods, evidence items, and individual residuals, without forcing all material into one universal schema.

### Open-Ended Question Intelligence
A proposed capacity to continue generating and exploring potentially useful questions as knowledge, methods, and contexts change. Open-ended generation is distinct from demonstrated knowledge growth, capability growth, RSI, or ASI.

### Operational Control Plane
The layer that checks permissions and executes authorized exploration tasks, monitors resources, and records outcomes. It must not infer authority solely from scores or strategic relevance.

### Perspective-Aware Scoring
Assessment that records whose perspective, objectives, assumptions, and evaluation criteria apply. Different perspectives can produce different legitimate scores without making evidence standards optional.

### Portfolio / Research Portfolio
A selected collection of research questions or explorations subject to resource budgets, constraints, dependencies, diversity considerations, and strategic priorities.

### Portfolio Control Plane
The layer that translates authorized research policy into allocation and selection constraints across a collection of candidate questions.

### Portfolio Selection
Selection of a set of questions rather than merely ranking individual questions, taking account of complementarities, redundancy, risk, budgets, and diversity. Selection is not execution authorization.

### Provenance
The traceable origin and transformation history of a question, relation, score, evidence item, decision, or relay artifact.

## Q — S

### Question Differential Tree (QDT)
A structured representation of related questions using shared cores, typed deltas, variants, and lineage, intended to support historical retrieval, transfer, and evolution. Tree organization is a design choice and may be supplemented by graph relations.

### Question Ecosystem (QE)
A changing network of questions, structural factors, evidence, methods, historical outcomes, dependencies, and relay relationships. Its health is not measured by question count alone.

### Question Expression Adapter
A mechanism that renders a canonical Question Unit into an executable or communicable form, such as an LLM prompt, API request, SQL query, experiment protocol, or formal proposition, without changing the question's canonical identity.

### Question Lifecycle
The versioned states and transitions of a Question Unit, such as candidate, evaluated, selected, exploring, explored, relayed, deferred, and revisited. State names and permitted transitions depend on the applicable contract.

### Question-to-Question Relay
A traceable transformation or relationship through which one or more questions lead to new questions, including derivation, decomposition, composition, transfer, or reconsideration.

### Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)
A process in which a system proposes changes to its own methods or components, evaluates those changes against independent criteria, and adopts them only after required validation and authorization. AQSI treats verified RSI as a research objective, not an achieved result.

### Relay Piece
A portable, typed artifact conveying a question, method, evidence, counter-evidence, mapping, result, or unresolved issue with provenance and applicable constraints.

### Repeated-Execution Relay
The reuse or repetition of an exploration that contributes new traceable information, such as replication evidence, drift detection, or improved measurement. Repetition without independent information should not inflate confidence.

### Residual / Residual-Driven Discovery
A *residual* is a documented gap between expected and observed outcomes, required and available capabilities, or solved and unresolved structure. Residual-driven discovery uses such gaps to generate new questions.

### Research Value
An evaluation-dependent judgment of a question's potential or demonstrated contribution. Estimated value, direct outcomes, delayed relay value, and verified scientific contribution should be reported separately.

### Scoring Contract
A specification of score semantics, inputs, directionality, evidence handling, uncertainty, folding, versioning, and comparability. See `SPEC-002-Structural-Influence-and-Scoring-Contract.md`.

### Strategic Question Control Plane (SQCP)
A governance mechanism by which authorized humans or institutions specify research directions, resource budgets, priorities, and constraints while leaving the discovery and tactical selection of specific questions to the AQSI system within those boundaries.

### Strategic Control Plane
The top-level authority defining why and where research resources may be used, subject to human or institutional governance. It does not require specifying each research question.

### Structural Change Proposal (SCP)
A documented proposal to modify a type system, scorer, relay operator, contract, or other structural component, with expected benefits, evidence, risks, compatibility checks, rollback conditions, and required approval.

### Structural Relay
Traceable transfer, adaptation, or accumulation of research structure across questions, explorations, domains, agents, or versions. Relay can convey useful negative findings and explicit leftovers, not only successful answers.

### Structure-First Question Generation
A path in which structural relationships, anomalies, missing links, or transfer candidates are identified first, then expressed as candidate research questions. It complements LLM-first generation.

## T — U

### Tactical Control Plane
The layer that proposes and scores specific questions, coordinates exploration strategies, and recommends portfolio candidates within strategic and portfolio constraints.

### Transfer Point
A specific typed feature, relation, method, or dependency that may support a qualified mapping from one research domain to another.

### Two-Phase Search
A design pattern that separates broad candidate localization or generation from more expensive validation, calibration, or detailed assessment. AQSI can use it for cost-aware screening without treating first-stage scores as definitive.

### UCPSF (Universal Control Planes and Scoring Folding)
A related structural-intelligence research framework addressing configurable control planes and scoring/folding operations. AQSI draws on these ideas while defining its own question-specific contracts.

### Unknown Relation
A relationship whose existence, sign, magnitude, or direction is not established. It must remain distinguishable from a measured zero or a verified absence of influence.

### UTN (Universal Typing and Naming)
A proposed shared typing, identity, naming, and semantic-alignment layer for heterogeneous structural objects. In AQSI it can help identify concepts, attributes, methods, evidence, relations, and cross-domain mappings while preserving type and context differences.

### UTN-Based Alignment
A typed mapping between objects or relations under declared semantic assumptions, transformation rules, and limits. Matching labels alone does not establish semantic equivalence.

## Key distinctions and non-equivalences

| Distinction | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Question Unit ≠ prompt | A persistent research object can have multiple expressions and executions. |
| Estimated influence ≠ causal proof | Directional scores express hypotheses or models until tested. |
| Unknown ≠ zero | Missing evidence must not be silently interpreted as no influence. |
| Question ranking ≠ portfolio optimization | The best set may differ from the top individual scores. |
| Question selection ≠ action authorization | A selected question still needs applicable permissions. |
| Relay acceptance ≠ scientific verification | A received artifact still requires assessment. |
| Repeated execution ≠ independent corroboration | Evidence dependence can invalidate naïve confidence gains. |
| Cross-domain question transfer ≠ answer transfer | Target-domain claims require target-domain validation. |
| Multi-agent interaction ≠ collective learning | Learning requires durable, reusable, evaluated changes. |
| Meta-question generation ≠ verified RSI | Proposed self-changes require independent tests and approval. |
| Open-ended questioning ≠ ASI | Continued question generation does not prove superior intelligence. |

## Maintenance and evolution

This is a **versioned vocabulary**, not a claim that one ontology can capture every research domain. New terms should be introduced with a definition, provenance, relationship to existing terms, known ambiguity, and, where relevant, a proposed UTN type or contract. Incompatible changes should be documented and reviewed rather than silently reinterpreting historical records.

For proposed experiments, see `TESTABLE-HYPOTHESES.md` and `VALIDATION-ROADMAP.md` when those repository files are available. For unresolved terminology and open design issues, see `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`.
